Decide how much of your account you are prepared to lose if the stop is hit, and work backwards to the position size that keeps the loss within it. Formulas

The calculator uses three steps.

  1. Amount at risk = account balance × risk % ÷ 100
  2. Value of 1 pip per lot = pip size × units per lot (× conversion rate)
  3. Lot size = amount at risk ÷ (stop loss in pips × value of 1 pip per lot)

The pip size is 0.01 for pairs quoted in Japanese yen (USD/JPY, EUR/JPY and so on) and 0.0001 for other pairs (EUR/USD, GBP/USD and so on). If the quote currency is the same as your account currency, no conversion is needed. Otherwise the pip value is converted with the rate between the quote currency and your account currency: for EUR/GBP in a USD account, P/L comes out in pounds and is multiplied by GBP/USD; for USD/JPY in a USD account, P/L comes out in yen and is divided by USD/JPY.

The result of step 3 is rounded down to 0.01 lots, so a stop-out at the size shown does not lose more than the amount at risk. If the calculated size is below the 0.01-lot minimum, 0.01 lots is shown together with a note that the loss would then exceed the amount at risk.

Worked examples

All figures are made-up examples for illustration.

Example 1: EUR/USD in a USD account

  • Balance 10,000 USD, risk 1%, stop loss 25 pips, 1 lot = 100,000 units
  • Amount at risk = 10,000 × 1 ÷ 100 = 100 USD
  • Value of 1 pip per lot = 0.0001 × 100,000 = 10 USD
  • Lot size = 100 ÷ (25 × 10) = 0.40 lots (40,000 units)

Example 2: USD/JPY in a USD account (with conversion)

  • Same balance, risk and stop; USD/JPY rate entered as 150.00
  • Value of 1 pip per lot = 0.01 × 100,000 ÷ 150 ≈ 6.67 USD (1,000 yen)
  • Lot size = 100 ÷ (25 × 6.667) = 0.60 lots (60,000 units)
  • Loss if the stop is hit at this size = 0.60 × 25 × 6.667 ≈ 100 USD

If your broker defines one lot as 10,000 units, the same risk gives 6 lots — the same 60,000 units. Always check what one lot means at your broker before placing an order.

Things to keep in mind

  • Units per lot differ between brokers. On MT4/MT5 a standard lot is usually 100,000 units, but mini and micro accounts exist, and some brokers use 10,000 units per lot. Check your broker's contract specifications.
  • Spreads, commissions and swaps are not included. In fast markets a stop order can also be filled at a worse price (slippage), so the actual loss can be larger than calculated.
  • The conversion rate is a snapshot. If the rate is different when the loss is realised, the amount in your account currency changes too.
  • Check margin separately. This calculator sizes the position from the loss you accept; it does not check the margin or leverage limits needed to open it.
  • There is no single right risk percentage. This page does not suggest a value; it only shows what the numbers you enter work out to.

FAQ

How many units are in one lot?
It depends on the broker and account type. On MT4/MT5 one standard lot is usually 100,000 units, with mini (10,000) and micro (1,000) lots on some accounts, and some brokers define one lot as 10,000 units. Check your broker's contract specifications. This calculator lets you choose the units per lot.
What risk percentage should I use?
There is no single right answer, and this calculator does not suggest a value. How much loss you can accept depends on your own situation and how often you trade. The calculator works out the lot size that keeps a stop-out within the percentage you enter.
Is anything I enter sent anywhere?
No. The calculation runs in your browser, and nothing you enter is sent or stored. Conversion rates are not fetched automatically; you enter them yourself.
