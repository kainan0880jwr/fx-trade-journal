A trading journal only helps if you keep using it, and on a phone the tool you pick decides a lot of that. This article is a checklist of what to look at before choosing a forex trading journal app for iPhone, from how quickly you can log a trade to where your records are stored and what happens if you stop paying. A checklist for choosing

1. How fast can you log a trade?

The main reason journals stop is that logging is a hassle, especially right after a loss. Try logging one trade during a free trial. If it takes more than a minute, you will probably skip entries on busy days.

2. Does it calculate pips and results for you?

Look for pips calculated from entry and exit prices, profit and loss in your account currency, and monthly totals such as win rate and profit factor. Check whether it handles the pip size of yen pairs and other pairs correctly.

3. Can it import your history?

If you trade on MT4 or MT5, importing a trade history report saves retyping. Check which formats are accepted (HTML reports, CSV), whether duplicates are skipped when you import the same file twice, and whether an import can be undone.

4. Where is your data stored, and who can see it?

Trade records reveal a lot about you. Check whether you need an account, whether records are sent to the developer's servers, whether they are encrypted on the device, and what the privacy policy says is collected. Read it rather than relying on the store listing.

5. Can you back up and move your records?

When you change phones, your records should come with you. Look for a backup file you control, ideally with the option of a password, and a way to export your trades (for example as CSV).

6. What analysis do you actually need?

Most traders start with monthly totals, results by pair and a calendar. Breakdowns by session, weekday, setup tag and risk-reward become useful once you have a few months of records. Check which of these are on the free plan.

7. What are the pricing terms?

For subscriptions, check the free trial length, how and when to cancel, and what happens to your records and which features stay available if the subscription ends.

How FX Trade Journal handles each point

FAQ

Do I need an account to use a trading journal app?
It depends on the app. Some require an account and store your records on their servers; others, including FX Trade Journal, need no account and keep records on your device. Check the app’s privacy policy before you start logging.
Can I move my records to a new iPhone?
In FX Trade Journal, you create a backup file in Settings and restore it on the new device. Other apps may use cloud sync instead; check how backup works before you rely on an app.
Is there an Android version?
The Android version of FX Trade Journal is in closed testing on Google Play. For now the app runs on iPhone and iPad (iOS 16.4 or later).
