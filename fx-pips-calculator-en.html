Pip size: JPY pairs vs other pairs

A pip is a unit for comparing price moves across currency pairs. How large one pip is depends on the quote currency (the right side of the pair).

Pair1 pipExample
Quoted in yen (USD/JPY, EUR/JPY, …)0.01150.00 → 150.50 is 50 pips
Other pairs (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, …)0.00011.0850 → 1.0880 is 30 pips

Most brokers quote one extra digit (150.123 on USD/JPY, 1.08505 on EUR/USD). That last digit is a tenth of a pip, often called a point or pipette (1 pip = 10 points). If MT4/MT5 shows a stop distance in points, divide by 10 to get pips.

Formulas

  1. Price move = exit − entry for a buy, entry − exit for a sell
  2. Pips = price move ÷ pip size (rounded to 0.1 pip)
  3. P/L in the quote currency = price move × lots × units per lot
  4. P/L in your account currency = step 3 converted with the rate between the quote currency and your account currency (no conversion if they are the same)

For example, EUR/GBP in a USD account is multiplied by GBP/USD, and USD/JPY in a USD account is divided by USD/JPY. In the second case, if you leave the conversion rate blank, the exit price is used.

Worked examples

All figures are made-up examples for illustration.

Example 1: buying EUR/USD in a USD account

  • Entry 1.0850, exit 1.0880, 1 lot (1 lot = 100,000 units)
  • Pips = (1.0880 − 1.0850) ÷ 0.0001 = +30.0 pips
  • P/L = 0.0030 × 1 × 100,000 = +300 USD

Example 2: selling USD/JPY in a USD account

  • Entry 150.00, exit 149.40, 1 lot (100,000 units), conversion rate left blank (exit price used)
  • Pips = (150.00 − 149.40) ÷ 0.01 = +60.0 pips
  • P/L in yen = 0.60 × 1 × 100,000 = +60,000 JPY
  • P/L in dollars = 60,000 ÷ 149.40 ≈ +401.61 USD

Example 3: buying EUR/GBP in a USD account

  • Entry 0.8600, exit 0.8625, 0.5 lots (100,000 units per lot), GBP/USD rate entered as 1.2700
  • Pips = (0.8625 − 0.8600) ÷ 0.0001 = +25.0 pips
  • P/L in pounds = 0.0025 × 0.5 × 100,000 = +125 GBP
  • P/L in dollars = 125 × 1.2700 ≈ +158.75 USD

Things to keep in mind

  • Commissions and swaps are not included. If you enter the prices you were actually filled at, the spread is already reflected; subtract per-trade commissions and swaps separately.
  • Units per lot differ between brokers. On MT4/MT5 a standard lot is usually 100,000 units, but check your broker's contract specifications.
  • The conversion rate matters. If your broker converts at a different rate from the one you enter, the account-currency P/L will differ slightly.
  • Some brokers count differently. The table above is the common convention; check how your broker defines pips and points.

FAQ

How much is one pip worth?
It depends on the pair and the position size. On EUR/USD and other pairs quoted in dollars, one pip is 0.0001, so it is worth 10 USD on 100,000 units and 1 USD on 10,000 units. On USD/JPY one pip is 0.01 yen, which is 1,000 yen on 100,000 units; in a USD account that is converted at the USD/JPY rate (about 6.67 USD at 150.00).
What is the difference between a pip and a point?
Many brokers quote prices one digit finer than a pip, and that smallest step is often called a point or pipette. In that case 1 pip = 10 points. If MT4/MT5 shows a stop distance in points, divide by 10 to get pips. Terminology varies, so check your broker's specifications.
Is anything I enter sent anywhere?
No. The calculation runs in your browser, and nothing you enter is sent or stored. Conversion rates are not fetched automatically; you enter them yourself.
