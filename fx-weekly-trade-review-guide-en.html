How to Review Your Forex Trades Each Week
A Simple Checklist

Published: November 27, 2026 Reading time: 7 min

This article explains how to keep and review your own forex trade records. It is not investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell anything. The methods described here are not a guarantee of profits or of a higher win rate, and all investment decisions are your own responsibility. All numbers in this article are fictional examples for illustration.

Most traders who keep a journal log their trades but rarely look back at them. A short, fixed weekly review is what turns a pile of entries into something you can learn from. This article lays out a routine that takes about 20–30 minutes: what to prepare, an eight-question checklist, which numbers to read and how, and how to finish with one concrete change for the week ahead.

Why review every week

A day is too short to see anything but noise, and a month is long enough that you forget why you took each trade. A week usually holds enough trades to compare, while your notes and memory of each one are still fresh. It also matches the trading week: markets close over the weekend, which gives you a natural time to step back.

The goal of a weekly review is not to judge whether the week was "good". It is to check whether you traded the way you planned, and to notice patterns early, before they cost you a month.

Before you start

  • Fix the time. The same slot every week (for example Saturday morning) makes it a habit rather than something you do after a bad week.
  • Make sure every trade is logged. Fill in any trades you skipped, including the losing ones. A review that leaves out the bad trades will always look better than reality.
  • Close the charts. Review what you did, not what the market is doing now. Looking at live prices pulls you into planning the next trade instead.

The eight-question checklist

Answer these in writing, briefly. One or two lines each is enough.

  1. How many trades did I take, and was that the number I intended? A jump in trade count is often the first sign of overtrading.
  2. Did every trade have a stop loss set at entry? List any that did not.
  3. Which trades broke one of my rules? Note which rule and what happened.
  4. What were my best and worst trades, and why? Look at the reasons, not the size of the result.
  5. Did I trade at times or on pairs I had planned to avoid?
  6. How did I feel before my losing trades? Tired, rushed, trying to win back a loss?
  7. Was there a setup I kept taking that did not work this week? One week is too few trades to drop it, but it is worth flagging.
  8. What one thing will I do differently next week? (See below.)

Which numbers to read

Numbers help you check your impressions against your record. Five are usually enough for a weekly review.

  • Number of trades — read it first; every other number is less reliable when it is small.
  • Win rate — the share of closed trades that ended in profit.
  • Average win and average loss (realized risk-reward) — average win ÷ average loss.
  • Profit factor (PF) — gross profit ÷ gross loss; above 1.0 means winners added up to more than losers for the period.
  • Results by pair and by session — where the week's result actually came from.

A fictional example: a week with 12 trades, 5 wins averaging 22 pips and 7 losses averaging 13 pips. The win rate is 5 ÷ 12 ≈ 41.7%, realized risk-reward is 22 ÷ 13 ≈ 1.69, and PF is (5 × 22) ÷ (7 × 13) = 110 ÷ 91 ≈ 1.21. The total is +19 pips. Read together, the week was positive because wins were larger than losses, not because most trades won. If the following week's losses grow while the win rate stays the same, that is where to look.

With only a week of trades, these numbers swing a lot. Treat them as a check against your written answers, and judge trends over several weeks rather than one. For how to calculate and read them, see "Forex Win Rate and Risk-Reward" and "What Is Profit Factor in Forex?".

Finish with one change

A review that ends with ten resolutions usually changes nothing. Pick one change for next week that you can check with a yes or no, and write it where you will see it before trading. For example:

  • "No new trade within 30 minutes of a loss."
  • "Set the stop loss before entering, every time."
  • "No more than three trades per day."

Next week, the first question of your review is whether you kept it. These examples are illustrations of checkable changes, not recommendations; the right change depends on what your own record shows.

Common mistakes

Reviewing only after a losing week

Skipping the review after a good week means you only ever study losses, and you miss whether good results came from following your plan or from luck.

Changing the strategy every week

A week rarely holds enough trades to tell whether a setup works. Flag it, keep logging, and decide after a month or more of the same conditions.

Reading results without the trade count

"Win rate 80%" from five trades and from fifty trades are very different statements. Always write the number of trades next to any rate.

Not logging the reason for each trade

Without a reason and a short note per trade, the checklist questions have nothing to work with. A tag for the setup and one line on how you felt is enough.

FAQ

How long should a weekly trade review take?
About 20 to 30 minutes is enough for most traders: a few minutes to make sure every trade is logged, about ten minutes for the checklist, and a few minutes to read the numbers and write down one change for next week.
What if I only traded a few times this week?
Do the review anyway, but focus on the checklist rather than the numbers. With only a few trades, rates like win rate swing too much to mean anything; whether you followed your rules is still worth checking.
Should I review daily as well?
A quick daily note (what you traded and how you felt) makes the weekly review much easier, but most traders find that drawing conclusions from a single day is too noisy. Keep daily notes short and save judgments for the weekly review.
